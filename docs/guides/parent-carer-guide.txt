# Parent and carer guide
+ If your child is thinking about a T Level, here's what you need to know,
+ and a few questions that are worth asking.

## What is a T Level?
A T Level is a 2-year course for 16 to 19 year olds, taken after GCSEs. It's a Level 3 qualification, the same size as 3 A levels. Each one focuses on a single area of work, such as Digital, Business, Finance or Engineering, so your child learns skills that employers actually use.

## How their time is spent
Most of the course, about 80%, is in the classroom. The other 20% is an industry placement with a real employer. The placement lasts at least 315 hours, which is about 45 days. Some colleges run it as one block, others as a day a week, and some mix the two.

## Will they be paid on placement?
Not necessarily. There's no legal requirement for employers to pay T Level students, although some choose to. It's worth asking the college what's usual for the placements they arrange.

## What about English and maths?
If your child hasn't got grade 4 in English and maths yet, they'll keep working towards it during the course. They don't need it to pass their T Level.

## What can it lead to?
- A skilled job
- A higher or degree apprenticeship
- A higher-level course, or university

## Questions worth asking the college
- How will you find my child's placement, and how will you support them while they're there?
- Who should we contact if there's a problem during the placement?
- How will we hear about how they're getting on?

> Information from TLevels.gov.uk and GOV.UK, correct in October 2026.
