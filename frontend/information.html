Information & Advice

Your questions, made clearer.

The practical details on eligibility, applications and life after a T Level. A useful place to start for students, parents and educators.

The essentials

Good to know before you start.

Who can study a T Level?

T Levels are primarily for 16–19 year olds in England after GCSEs. Entry requirements are set by each school or college and may vary by course. Ask your provider about the grades and subjects you need.

How do I apply?

Choose a subject, find a provider and apply directly to that school or college. Check their deadlines, attend an open day and ask how they organise industry placements. You do not apply for a T Level through UCAS.

Is a T Level the same as an apprenticeship?

No. A T Level is mainly college- or school-based and includes an industry placement. An apprenticeship is a paid job with training. They are different routes to developing technical skills.

How long is the industry placement?

Most T Levels include at least 315 hours of industry placement, around 45 days. Early Years Educator placements require at least 750 hours. Your provider will explain the requirements for your course.

Do I get paid on placement?

Payment is not compulsory for an industry placement. Some employers may offer pay or help with costs. Ask your provider about travel, meals and any financial support before you start.

What if I am not ready for a T Level yet?

Ask a provider about the T Level Foundation Year. It is a one-year study programme that can help you prepare for a T Level, where available.

UCAS Tariff points

A qualification that can take you further.

T Levels attract UCAS Tariff points. The number depends on your overall grade, including the core and occupational specialism.

University entry requirements vary. Check that your chosen course accepts your specific T Level and any required subjects.

After your T Level

Your next chapter has options.

  • Skilled employment

    Use your technical knowledge and placement experience to apply for relevant entry-level roles.

  • Higher education

    Explore degrees and higher technical qualifications. Acceptance depends on the course and provider.

  • Apprenticeships

    Consider a higher or degree apprenticeship, subject to the employer’s entry and recruitment requirements.
