# T Levels explained

Sources: TLevels.gov.uk (Students, About T Levels), checked 2026-10-20;
UCAS (T Levels page), checked 2026-10-20. Always tell users to confirm details
with TLevels.gov.uk or their school or college.

## What a T Level is

- A T Level is a 2-year qualification you can take in England after your GCSEs.
- The government developed T Levels with employers.
- There are more than 20 T Level subjects. Each one covers a broad industry
  area and lets you specialise.
- One T Level is worth the same as 3 A levels.
- There are no tuition fees if you start before you turn 19, the same as
  A levels.
- If you need more preparation first, there is a 1-year T Level Foundation
  Year.

## How your time is split

- About 80% of the course is in the classroom at school or college.
- About 20% is an industry placement with an employer.
- The industry placement is 45 days. When it happens depends on the T Level,
  the school or college, and the employer.
- A T Level is around 1,800 hours of study over two years (UCAS).

## Grades and UCAS points

You get an overall grade of Pass, Merit, Distinction or Distinction*, and a
nationally recognised certificate.

| Overall grade | UCAS Tariff points |
|---|---|
| Distinction* | 168 |
| Distinction | 144 |
| Merit | 120 |
| Pass (C or above on the core) | 96 |
| Pass (D or E on the core) | 72 |

A Distinction* counts the same as three A* grades at A level.

## After a T Level

- Skilled work in the industry you studied.
- A higher apprenticeship.
- University or other higher education. Not every university course accepts
  T Levels; the government publishes a list of providers that do.

## How to find and apply for a T Level

- You apply through the school or college that teaches it, not through UCAS.
- You can search for T Levels near you on TLevels.gov.uk by postcode or town.
- Entry requirements are set by each school or college.

## Where to get personal advice

- Your school or college careers adviser.
- The National Careers Service.
- TLevels.gov.uk.
